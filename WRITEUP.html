Qalaisyn: an engine that lets AI understand Kazakh the way people really type it

Track: Languages · Team: Zhasulan B. · Try it live (in your browser): zembono.github.io/qalaisyn/web · Code: github.com/ZemBono/qalaisyn

The problem

Most phones in Kazakhstan have a Russian keyboard with no keys for the Kazakh letters ә ғ қ ң ө ұ ү һ і, so people type the closest Russian letter: калайсын for қалайсың. One letter can change the meaning: өт "go through" becomes от "fire". In our tests almost every second word typed this way is misspelled, and the AI that now answers bank, airline, clinic and government chats misreads it. An airline bot's translator reads ушак кешигип жатыр ма ("is the plane late?") as "is there a rumor going on?". A bank bot asked несие бойынша толемди кешиктирсем бола ма ("can I delay my loan payment?") explains how to withdraw cash.

What we built

Qalaisyn is an engine that works inside other systems: it sits in a company's system, between the incoming message and the AI that answers it, like a spell-checker inside a keyboard. In: баламнын кызуы 39 котерилди не истеу керек. Out: баламның қызуы 39 көтерілді не істеу керек ("my child's temperature rose to 39, what should I do"), in Cyrillic or the new official Latin alphabet. When it is unsure it shows suggestions (от 73% · өт 27%) instead of guessing silently. It handles Russian-keyboard and Latin typing, leaves Russian words alone, and runs in about 7 ms per message (1.3 GB of memory). It also runs as a browser extension: Alt+Q in any text field, e.g. Google Translate. User: Gulnar, 62, from a village in Turkistan region, texting a clinic chatbot at night from a phone with only a Russian keyboard.

What we added

Our own data and questions: a generator of six real typing styles, 70 real messages written by a native speaker (one person, with permission), stress tests asking when does it break?, calibrated suggestions, a "why not a dictionary?" study with confidence intervals, and a transfer test on Kyrgyz.

How it works

Data: 1.5M Kazakh Wikipedia sentences plus conversational text (Tatoeba, film subtitles). Held-out tests: 623 everyday and 300–1,012 news sentences (FLORES-200), each with a professional English translation, in six typing styles. Tuning on FLORES dev only. Model: each typed word → the Kazakh words it could be → a typing-habit model and a word-bigram language model score them → Viterbi picks the best sentence; a character 5-gram model handles unknown words, a one-edit search handles typos, and forward-backward gives each word a probability (below 90% → suggestion).

Why a model and not a dictionary? A chatbot needs whole messages right; Qalaisyn fixes 154 of the dictionary's word mistakes for 38 new ones, wins by 21–40 points on messy messages, and knows when to ask. Why not a large AI model? It is paid per message, takes seconds and sends customers' messages to another company; Qalaisyn is free, takes milliseconds, runs on the company's own server and can sit in front of any AI (and on the native messages it is more accurate than free ChatGPT, 11.9% vs 12.9%; 16.1% before two rules added after reading those errors). Prior work: restoring dropped letters has been studied for languages such as Vietnamese and Romanian; new here is chat-style Kazakh (Russian keyboard or Latin, slang, Russian words, typos) with calibrated suggestions. Honest notes: on clean news text translation scores are equal (CI includes 0). Kyrgyz with the same code recovers 98% of the meaning in Latin and 86% on a Russian keyboard (where a dictionary is equally good). Where it fails: н/ң and і/и when both words exist, true ambiguity (от/өт), and some colloquial forms.

What we'd do next

  1. More chat forms (кейн → кейін, келейн → келейін), more native-speaker test messages, and longer context for н/ң and і/и. 2. A plug-in API for support chatbots and a phone keyboard; more languages.
