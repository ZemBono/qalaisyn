Qalaisyn: making AI understand Kazakh the way people actually text

Track: Languages · Team: Zhasulan B.

The problem

Most Kazakhs text in Latin letters and drop the special Kazakh letters (ә, ғ, қ, ң, ө, ұ, ү, і): "qalaisyn" instead of "қалайсың". Translators and chatbots are trained on standard Cyrillic Kazakh. We measured what that costs: a translator loses 75% of the meaning of chat-style Kazakh (chrF 60.8 → 15.3). As banks, clinics and e-government services put AI first in line, people who write "the way they text" get worse help, or none. The same problem affects other languages whose special letters get dropped on phones (Uzbek, Kyrgyz, Azerbaijani, Vietnamese, Yoruba…).

What we built

Qalaisyn is a small AI layer that sits in front of any translator or chatbot. In: a chat-style message such as balamnyn qyzuy 39 koterildi ne isteu kerek. Out: standard Kazakh, баламның қызуы 39 көтерілді не істеу керек ("my child's temperature rose to 39, what should I do"), which the AI can now understand. User: Gulnar, 62, from a village in Turkistan region. Her phone has no Kazakh keyboard, and she texts a clinic chatbot at night.

What we added

Our own idea (not a suggested project). Our own data and question: (1) a generator that simulates four real typing styles, so we never need anyone's private chats; (2) stress tests that ask when does it break? (typos and spellings the model was never designed for); (3) real messages written and corrected by a native speaker.

How it works

Data: 1.5M sentences from Kazakh Wikipedia and 4k from Tatoeba for training. Held-out tests: 623 everyday Tatoeba sentences and FLORES-200 news sentences, each with a professional English translation, turned into 4 chat styles. Tuning used only FLORES dev; no test sentence was seen in training. Model: For every chat word we generate the Kazakh words it could be (k → к/қ, a → а/ә, i → і/и/ы …). A typing-habit model (a writer who uses q for қ probably means к by k) and a word-bigram language model score them, and Viterbi decoding picks the most likely sentence. Unknown words are restored with a character 5-gram model, and probable typos with a one-edit neighbour search. It runs in milliseconds on a laptop, and every decision can be explained.

Honest notes. On clean news text, a simple dictionary is already as good as our model. Our advantage appears on everyday and messy messages, which is what real chats look like. Typo search costs about 1.5 points of word errors on perfectly clean text; we accept that cost because it adds 15 chrF points on unseen spellings. Where it fails: н/ң and і/ы/и (both choices are often real words, and a bigram has short memory), and true ambiguity like ot = от "fire" / өт "pass". Some "errors" are actually typos in the reference (қагаз, сатыл ал), which Qalaisyn fixed to қағаз, сатып ал, so our scores are slightly pessimistic. Transfer: the same code, trained only on Kyrgyz Wikipedia, recovers 97% of the lost meaning for Kyrgyz (word errors 3.3% vs. 6.3% for the dictionary). Native-speaker check: see notebook section 9.

What we'd do next

  1. Use longer context (trigrams or a small neural re-ranker) for н/ң and і/ы/и.
  2. Add more languages (Uzbek, Azerbaijani) and offer Qalaisyn as a plug-in for public-service chatbots.
